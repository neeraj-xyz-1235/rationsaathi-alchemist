official problem statement :-
P-01 — RationSaathi: Public Distribution Entitlement & Dispute Assistant Domain: 6 — Public Systems & Civic Technology Problem Overview Public distribution schemes provide subsidised goods to eligible households at set quantities for set periods. Collections may be partial or spread over several visits, receipts may be lost or hard to read, and beneficiaries often have no simple way to compare what they are owed with what they received. A clear, evidence-based comparison can resolve simple recording errors and make a grievance easier to review. The calculation itself is simple, but its inputs are messy, and a discrepancy may have benign causes, such as genuine stock shortages. A shortfall is not evidence of wrongdoing, and presenting it as one would cause real harm. The Challenge Build a tool that compares a beneficiary’s expected entitlement with the quantities actually received, and prepares a structured discrepancy report. • Entitlements must be expressed through explicit rules the user enters, such as a quantity per member per period. Apply only the rules provided; do not assume others. • Record distributions, including partial deliveries spread over a period, and calculate shortfalls with exact arithmetic that the user can check line by line. • Link every record to its source, such as a note, receipt reference, or pasted receipt text, and keep that evidence in the report. • Receipt text may be interpreted to extract quantities and dates, and a discrepancy may be classified (for example, shortfall, late, unrecorded, or unclear). Extracted values are suggestions the user must confirm, and arithmetic must not depend on AI. • The report must use neutral language, describe the difference and its evidence, and leave the cause open. It must never state or imply that anyone has committed fraud.

In simple words

You are building a ration calculator and report maker.

The situation: The government gives families fixed amounts of food each month. For example, 5 kg of rice per person. Families often collect it in pieces, lose receipts, and can’t tell if they got everything they were owed.

Your tool answers one question: “How much should this family have received, how much did they actually get, and what is the difference?”

What the user does, step by step
Enters the rule. “Rice: 5 kg per person per month.”
Enters the family. “4 members.”
Adds what they received, with proof for each entry:
3rd Oct: 8 kg rice (Receipt #101)
20th Oct: 6 kg rice (Receipt #145)
They can also paste messy receipt text like “Rice 8kg recvd 03/10”, and your tool reads the quantity and date out of it.
Confirms what the tool read from the receipt text. It is only a suggestion until they approve it.
Gets a report:
Should have received: 4 × 5 = 20 kg
Actually received: 8 + 6 = 14 kg
Difference: 6 kg less than entitled
Evidence: Receipt #101, Receipt #145
Type: shortfall
The report must be neutral

It says “there is a 6 kg difference.” It does not say “someone cheated,” because the cause could be a genuine stock shortage. The cause stays open.

Where the AI goes

You need two small AI parts:

Reading receipt text and pulling out quantity and date.
Labelling the difference as shortfall, late, unrecorded, or unclear.

The math is plain code, with no AI. The booklet requires this.

What you actually build
Part	What it is
Input screen	Rules, family size, distribution records
Receipt reader	Extracts quantity and date from text (AI, with a simple fallback)
Calculator	Normal Python arithmetic, shown line by line
Classifier	Labels each gap
Report page	Neutral wording, with evidence links

Overall it’s a simple web app: a form, a calculator, a small AI part, and a clean report.

# RationSaathi — Frontend Development Plan

## 1. Project Overview

**Project Name:** RationSaathi  
**Domain:** Public Systems & Civic Technology  
**Problem Statement:** P-01 — RationSaathi: Public Distribution Entitlement & Dispute Assistant  
**Frontend Technology:** React.js, Vite, Tailwind CSS  
**Target Languages:** English, Hindi, Marathi  
**Development Timeline:** 5–6 hours for the initial frontend prototype  
**Primary Objective:** Build a simple, accessible, multilingual, and trustworthy web application that helps ration beneficiaries compare their expected entitlement against the quantities they have actually received and generate an evidence-based discrepancy report.

### 1.1 Project Vision

RationSaathi should make it easy for a beneficiary to answer three questions:

1. How much ration should my family receive?
2. How much ration have we actually received?
3. Is there any difference between the expected and recorded quantities?

The application should work for people with different levels of digital literacy. Users should not need technical knowledge to navigate the interface, enter ration details, understand calculations, or generate a report.

The interface should prioritize clarity and accessibility over unnecessary complexity.

### 1.2 Core Design Principle

**A user should be able to check their ration entitlement without needing technical knowledge.**

The application must be easy to understand, visually organized, multilingual, responsive, and transparent about how every quantity is calculated.

A discrepancy must never automatically be treated as evidence of fraud or wrongdoing. Reports should describe the recorded quantities, differences, and supporting evidence while leaving the cause open.

---

## 2. Design Direction and Visual Identity

The design should take inspiration from the clean layouts, reusable components, consistent visual hierarchy, polished cards, and responsive interface patterns used in the VisionAttend project.

However, RationSaathi should have its own identity.

VisionAttend uses a futuristic AI-oriented interface. RationSaathi should instead feel trustworthy, welcoming, approachable, and appropriate for a public-service application.

### 2.1 Design Style

- Clean, modern, minimal, and professional.
- Government-service-oriented visual identity.
- Large buttons and easily readable text.
- Familiar icons with clear labels.
- Rounded cards and generous spacing.
- Simple forms with visible labels and instructions.
- Subtle animations and transitions.
- Mobile-first responsive design.
- Minimal technical terminology.
- Consistent layout across all languages.

Avoid excessive gradients, complex dashboards, unnecessary animations, tiny text, crowded layouts, and technical-looking interfaces that may confuse users.

### 2.2 Color Palette

Use a consistent palette throughout the application.

| Element | Color | Hex Code |
|---|---|---|
| Primary color | Trust green | #176B45 |
| Main background | Soft green-white | #F3F7F2 |
| Card background | White | #FFFFFF |
| Accent color | Warm amber | #E9A23B |
| Primary text | Dark charcoal | #1F2937 |
| Secondary text | Muted gray | #6B7280 |
| Borders | Light gray | #E5E7EB |

Color usage:

- Green: primary buttons, progress indicators, and positive interface elements.
- Amber: attention indicators and items requiring review.
- Gray: secondary information, neutral observations, and supporting text.
- Red: reserved for actual errors or critical validation problems, not automatically for ration discrepancies.

A shortfall should be displayed neutrally rather than using alarming colors or language that implies misconduct.

### 2.3 Typography

- Use a clean, readable font.
- Keep body text at approximately 16 px or larger wherever practical.
- Use clear heading sizes and consistent font weights.
- Ensure Hindi and Marathi Devanagari characters render correctly.
- Use a font stack with reliable Devanagari support, such as Noto Sans Devanagari when available.
- Avoid decorative fonts for essential information.

### 2.4 Component Styling

All pages should use a shared design system.

Reusable components should have consistent:

- Border radius.
- Spacing.
- Button height.
- Typography.
- Colors.
- Focus states.
- Hover states.
- Disabled states.
- Validation messages.
- Loading indicators.

Buttons and important form controls should ideally have a minimum height of 48 px.

## 3. Target Users and Accessibility

The application is intended for ration beneficiaries with varying levels of digital literacy.

The interface must not assume that every user is comfortable with websites, forms, English terminology, or technical instructions.

### 3.1 Accessibility Requirements

1. Use large, clearly labelled buttons.
2. Use explicit form labels instead of relying only on placeholders.
3. Keep instructions short and direct.
4. Use icons together with text rather than icons alone.
5. Provide clear error messages explaining what needs to be corrected.
6. Support keyboard navigation and visible focus indicators.
7. Maintain sufficient color contrast.
8. Avoid unnecessary pop-ups and complicated menus.
9. Display quantities with their units, such as kg.
10. Keep the primary action visible and easy to identify.
11. Do not communicate important information through color alone.
12. Use simple language and avoid unexplained technical terms.

### 3.2 Guided User Journey

Instead of presenting a complicated dashboard, use a guided, numbered workflow.

The user should move through the following steps:

1. Enter family details and entitlement rules.
2. Add distribution records and supporting evidence.
3. Review the calculated entitlement and received quantities.
4. View, print, or save the discrepancy report.

Each step should have clear Next and Back actions.

The interface should preserve entered information when the user moves between steps.

### Screen B: Entitlement and Distribution Entry

**Purpose:** Collect the information needed to calculate the expected entitlement and recorded distributions.

This screen should follow a guided form layout.

Fields:

- Number of family members.
- Entitlement period start date.
- Entitlement period end date.

Allow the user to define the rules explicitly.

- Quantity per member: 5 kg.
- Entitlement period: One month.

It must not assume government entitlement rules or automatically introduce unverified eligibility criteria.

- Add a record.

- Item: Rice.

- Date: 3 October.

The interface must clearly identify these values as suggestions.

- Family size.

### Screen C: Results and Discrepancy Report

**Purpose:** Present a clear calculation and generate a structured report that the user can review, print, or save.

#### Summary cards

Display three prominent summary cards:

1. Expected entitlement.
2. Total recorded quantity received.
3. Difference between expected and received quantities.

Example:

- Expected: 20 kg.
- Received: 14 kg.
- Difference: 6 kg.

The displayed values must be calculated from the user's confirmed input data, not from hardcoded demonstration values.

#### Calculation breakdown

For the example above:

- Family members: 4.
- Entitlement rule: 5 kg per member.
- Expected entitlement: 4 × 5 = 20 kg.
- Receipt #101: 8 kg.
- Receipt #145: 6 kg.
- Total recorded quantity: 8 + 6 = 14 kg.
- Difference: 20 − 14 = 6 kg.

Show the calculation line by line so that the user can understand how the result was obtained.

#### Evidence section

For every distribution record, show:

- Date.
- Ration item.
- Quantity.
- Receipt reference or note.
- Original receipt text, when applicable.
- Whether the values were manually entered or extracted and confirmed.

Every record included in the calculation must be traceable to its source.

Do not imply that a receipt exists when the user has not provided one.

#### Discrepancy classification

The report may use the following classifications:

- **Shortfall:** Recorded quantity is lower than the expected entitlement.
- **Late:** A distribution was recorded after the relevant expected date or period, based on an explicit date or rule.
- **Unrecorded:** A receipt or distribution is mentioned in available evidence but has not been represented by a confirmed distribution record.
- **Unclear:** The available information is insufficient to classify the discrepancy confidently.

Classification rules must be transparent and should not rely on unsupported assumptions.

For example, a shortfall can be calculated from the difference between expected and recorded quantities. However, the application cannot conclude that the difference was caused by misconduct.

A distribution should not be labelled late unless an applicable expected date or deadline has been provided or defined.

#### Neutral report language

Example:

“The recorded distributions total 14 kg against an expected entitlement of 20 kg, resulting in a recorded difference of 6 kg. The reason for this difference has not been established.”

The report must never state or imply that a person has committed fraud, theft, or wrongdoing.

#### Report actions

Provide the following actions:

- Print Report.
- Save as PDF using the browser's print dialog.
- Copy Report Text.
- Return to Edit Details.

For the initial prototype, browser printing and copying report text are sufficient.

Use print-specific CSS to hide navigation and unnecessary controls when printing.

The interface should allow a user to provide an alternative source, such as a note or reference.

If evidence is missing:

- Do not invent a receipt number.
- Do not claim that the entry is receipt-backed.
- Display the evidence status accurately.
- Allow the report to explain that the entry was manually recorded or that its source is unclear.

A household may receive ration in multiple deliveries during the same period.

The calculator must aggregate all confirmed distributions for each item and period.

Example:

- Expected entitlement: 20 kg.
- First delivery: 8 kg.
- Second delivery: 6 kg.
- Total recorded: 14 kg.
- Difference: 6 kg.

Do not treat the first partial delivery as the final result if additional deliveries have been recorded.

The report should make clear that the calculation reflects the records available at the time it was generated.

## 7. Calculation Logic

The calculation must use ordinary application code. AI must not calculate, modify, or independently determine the final arithmetic result.

### 7.1 Expected Entitlement

For a per-member entitlement rule:

Expected quantity = Number of family members × Quantity per member for the relevant period.

Example:

`4 × 5 kg = 20 kg`

For more complex rules, the calculator should apply only the explicit rule provided by the user.

### 7.2 Total Recorded Quantity

For each ration item:

Total received = Sum of all confirmed distribution quantities associated with that item and entitlement period.

Example:

`8 kg + 6 kg = 14 kg`

### 7.3 Difference

Difference = Expected entitlement − Total recorded quantity.

Example:

`20 kg − 14 kg = 6 kg`

Interpretation:

- Positive difference: recorded quantity is lower than expected.
- Zero difference: recorded quantity equals expected entitlement.
- Negative difference: recorded quantity exceeds expected entitlement.

A negative difference should not automatically be treated as an error or wrongdoing. The report should display the actual signed difference and provide a clear explanation.

### 7.4 Exact Arithmetic

Do not rely on unrounded floating-point arithmetic for quantities.

Prefer integer base units where possible.

For example, store quantities in grams when the supported precision is one gram, or use a decimal arithmetic library when fractional quantities require a different precision.

The same units must be used when adding, subtracting, and comparing quantities.

Do not silently round values in a way that changes the reported difference.

### 7.5 Suggested Calculator File

`src/utils/calculator.js`

This file should contain pure calculation functions for:

- Calculating expected entitlement.
- Summing confirmed distributions.
- Calculating the difference.
- Generating line-by-line calculation details.
- Grouping records by ration item and entitlement period.

Keep calculation logic separate from the UI components so that it can be tested independently.

## 8. AI and Receipt Processing

The frontend should support AI-assisted receipt interpretation without making AI responsible for final calculations.

### 8.1 Receipt Reader

The receipt reader accepts pasted text and returns suggested fields.

Example input:

`Rice 8kg recvd 03/10`

Possible extracted values:

- Item: Rice.
- Quantity: 8 kg.
- Date: 3 October.

The extraction mechanism may use a backend AI endpoint, a local parser, or both.

The frontend should not assume that AI extraction is always correct.

### 8.2 Confirmation Workflow

The user must be able to:

1. Paste the receipt text.
2. Request extraction.
3. Review the suggested item, quantity, and date.
4. Edit incorrect fields.
5. Confirm the values.
6. Save the record.

Unconfirmed suggestions must not be included in the final calculation.

### 8.3 Fallback Parser

Create:

`src/utils/receiptParser.js`

The fallback parser should use simple patterns to identify common quantities and dates from pasted text.

For the first prototype, support a limited range of formats and clearly indicate when parsing is uncertain.

For example:

- `Rice 8kg recvd 03/10`
- `Wheat 5 kg received 12/10`

The parser should not assume a date format when the interpretation is ambiguous. For example, `03/10` could represent different dates depending on the format and year.

Request clarification when the date or quantity cannot be reliably interpreted.

### 8.4 AI Integration Boundary

The frontend should be designed so the receipt extraction service can be connected later.

A possible API interface is:

`POST /api/receipt/parse`

The request may contain the pasted receipt text and relevant context.

The response should contain suggested item, quantity, unit, date, and any confidence or ambiguity information supported by the backend.

This endpoint is a proposed interface, not an assumption that the backend has already implemented it.

During frontend development, use a local mock function or fallback parser until the real endpoint is available.

### 8.5 Classification

The frontend may display a classification returned by the backend or calculate it using transparent deterministic rules.

The classification must be based on explicit information and must not assert fraud or wrongdoing.

AI may help interpret ambiguous text, but it must not override confirmed user data or the deterministic calculation.

## 13. Demonstration Scenario

Use one consistent scenario to test the entire application.

### Input

Family size: 4 members.

Entitlement rule:

Rice = 5 kg per member for the specified month.

Distribution records:

- 3 October: 8 kg of rice, Receipt #101.
- 20 October: 6 kg of rice, Receipt #145.

### Expected Result

Expected entitlement:

`4 × 5 kg = 20 kg`

Recorded distribution total:

`8 kg + 6 kg = 14 kg`

Difference:

`20 kg − 14 kg = 6 kg`

Classification:

Shortfall, if the records establish that the applicable entitlement period has ended and the confirmed quantities are complete enough for that comparison.

Otherwise, the report should clearly state that the current recorded total is below the expected amount and that the final period outcome may be unresolved.

### Example Report

**RationSaathi — Entitlement Discrepancy Report**

- Expected entitlement: 20 kg.
- Total recorded quantity: 14 kg.
- Recorded difference: 6 kg.
- Supporting records: Receipt #101 and Receipt #145.

**Observation:**

“The recorded distributions total 14 kg against an expected entitlement of 20 kg, resulting in a recorded difference of 6 kg. The reason for this difference has not been established.”

This demonstration shows the core value of the application: understandable calculations, traceable evidence, and neutral reporting.

## 15. Implementation Principles

The following principles should guide all frontend development.

1. **Simplicity first:** Every screen must have an obvious purpose.
2. **Accessibility by design:** Build for users with different levels of digital literacy.
3. **Multilingual from the beginning:** Do not postpone translations until the end.
4. **Deterministic calculations:** Never delegate final arithmetic to AI.
5. **Evidence traceability:** Every distribution record must identify its source or explicitly indicate that evidence is missing.
6. **User confirmation:** AI-extracted values must be reviewed before they are used.
7. **Neutral reporting:** A numerical difference is not proof of wrongdoing.
8. **Responsive design:** All essential workflows must work on smaller screens.
9. **Reusable components:** Maintain a consistent UI without duplicating code.
10. **Time-boxed development:** Complete the working user journey before adding optional polish.
11. **Graceful uncertainty:** If a receipt, date, quantity, or classification is unclear, ask for clarification rather than inventing information.
12. **Clear ownership of logic:** Keep the UI, calculation logic, parsing logic, and backend integration separate.
